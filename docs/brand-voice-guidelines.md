# Voice and language guidelines

How the Old South End Partnership sounds, in English and in Spanish. Derived from the copy in the partnership's design canvas (September 2026); refine it with the marketing/communications committee.

## Who we're talking to

Old South End neighbors first: homeowners, renters, and families, many of them Spanish-speaking. Then funders, partners, the press, and the City. Write so a neighbor reading on a phone understands every sentence on the first read.

## Voice

- **Plain and warm.** "Neighbors can find, keep, and strengthen the homes they love." Not "residents can access housing stabilization resources."
- **We, together.** The partnership speaks as "we" and "the partners". Credit the specific partner when one did the work ("Led by the Lucas County Land Bank…").
- **Concrete over abstract.** Numbers, streets, and named events: "17 sidewalk replacements on Knower Street," "900 parcels," "Rock the Block."
- **Honest about stage.** "We're just getting started." Do not overstate results; every figure carries an as-of date.
- **No jargon without a gloss.** Where a term of art is needed (RISE area, receivership, tangled title), say what it means or keep it attached to a plain description.

## Spanish

- **Formal "usted"** throughout: "Conozca a los socios," "Contáctenos," "¿Tiene una pregunta?"
- Translate meaning, not words. "Get in Touch" is "Contáctenos"; "What's Next" is "Próximos pasos."
- Use opening punctuation (¿ ¡) and Spanish date order ("14 de mayo de 2026"); months are lowercase.
- Partnership is "la alianza" in running text and "la Old South End Partnership" with its name.
- Content that exists only in English (news stories) is labeled: "Leer el artículo (en inglés)."
- Every English change ships with its Spanish change in the same PR. If a translation must wait, say so in the PR and record it in `docs/TECH_DEBT.md`; never leave English text on the Spanish page silently.

## Formatting

- Sentence case for headings and labels; the uppercase eyebrows are CSS, not typed capitals.
- Curly quotes and apostrophes (’ “ ”), en dashes for ranges (7–10, 2025–2026).
- Numerals for counts (2 new homes, 315 properties) as in the design.
